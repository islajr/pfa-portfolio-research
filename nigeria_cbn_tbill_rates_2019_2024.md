# Average Nigerian CBN Treasury Bill Rates, 2019–2024

*Prepared for undergraduate dissertation reference. All data sourced from primary CBN publications and verified financial reporting. See References section for full APA 7th edition citations.*

---

## Background

Nigerian Treasury Bills (NTBs) are short-term debt instruments issued by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) on behalf of the Federal Government of Nigeria. They are issued at a discount and redeemed at par value on maturity. The CBN conducts primary market auctions (PMAs) typically on a fortnightly basis, offering bills across three standard tenors: 91-day, 182-day, and 364-day instruments (First Bank of Nigeria, n.d.; Afrinvest Asset Management, n.d.). The 364-day (one-year) bill is the benchmark most commonly used for fixed-income market analysis.

> **Methodological note:** A single authoritative full-year published average does not exist for every year in a single source. The 2020–2024 figures are drawn directly from the Stanbic IBTC Asset Management (2024) NTB market data sheet, which sources from the CBN. Researchers requiring exact monthly auction-by-auction data for 2019 should consult the CBN's official publications at [https://www.cbn.gov.ng](https://www.cbn.gov.ng).

## Contextual Notes by Year

### 2019
The CBN's Monetary Policy Rate (MPR) was reduced from 14% to 13.5% in March 2019, signalling a shift away from the high-yield environment that had prevailed since 2016 (Nairametrics, 2022). NTB rates declined broadly through the year. The 364-day stop rate peaked at approximately 12.94% in October 2019 before continuing to fall (Nairametrics, 2022). This was consistent with the CBN's explicit policy of channelling liquidity toward domestic credit rather than attracting foreign portfolio inflows via high T-bill yields.

### 2020
This was the most anomalous year in the period under review. The CBN adopted a near-zero interest rate stance on NTBs, driving the 364-day stop rate to an all-time low of 0.233% in November 2020 (CEIC, 2024). The 91-day and 364-day full-year averages were both approximately 1.69% (Stanbic IBTC Asset Management, 2024). This reflected the CBN's heterodox policy of forcing credit into the real economy by making T-bills unattractive relative to loans, coinciding with the economic contraction caused by COVID-19 and the collapse in oil revenues.

### 2021
The market began a gradual normalisation. While the 91-day rate remained suppressed at approximately 2.10%, the 364-day average recovered to approximately 7.01% as the CBN began allowing longer-tenor yields to drift higher (Stanbic IBTC Asset Management, 2024). Auction data from NigeriaGalleria (2022) shows the 364-day stop rate ranged from a low of 1.15% in January to a high of approximately 9.75% by May, before declining again toward year-end.

### 2022
Inflation accelerated sharply, prompting the CBN to reverse course. The MPR was raised from 11.5% to 15.5% across three consecutive MPC meetings (Nairametrics, 2022b). The 364-day T-bill rate rose to 12% in September 2022—its highest level since October 2019 (Nairametrics, 2022a). The full-year 364-day average was approximately 8.79%, with the 91-day averaging approximately 3.62% (Stanbic IBTC Asset Management, 2024). Auction records show the 364-day stop rate ranging from a low of 4.45% in March to a high of 13.99% in December (NigeriaGalleria, 2023a).

### 2023
The CBN continued tightening but NTB rates remained highly volatile and well below the headline inflation rate, implying deeply negative real yields for most of the year. The full-year 364-day average was approximately 10.06%, while the 91-day averaged approximately 4.44% (Stanbic IBTC Asset Management, 2024). Auction-level data from NigeriaGalleria (2023b) shows the 364-day stop rate ranging from approximately 4.89% in June to 16.75% in November and December, reflecting a significant acceleration in the second half of the year as the new CBN governor, Olayemi Cardoso, began signalling a more orthodox monetary policy stance.

### 2024 (YTD October)
This was the highest-rate environment since at least 2017. The CBN raised the MPR by a cumulative 870 basis points to 27.50% during 2024, directly driving NTB stop rates to multi-year highs (Kolaking/ThisDay, 2024). The 364-day stop rate spiked to 19% in February 2024—the highest since the April 2017 peak of 18.83% (Nairametrics, 2024)—and subsequently reached 23.5% in November 2024 (Kolaking/ThisDay, 2024). Year-to-date (through October 2024), the 364-day secondary market average was approximately 14.61% and the 91-day approximately 15.37% (Stanbic IBTC Asset Management, 2024). Total NTB issuance in 2024 reached approximately ₦13.3 trillion, a ~99.7% increase over the ₦6.66 trillion raised in 2023 (Allafrica/ThisDay, 2025).

## Data Limitations and Caveats for Academic Use

1. **No single consolidated source**: The CBN does not publish a single annual average T-bill rate table. The averages above are derived from secondary market summaries (Stanbic IBTC Asset Management, 2024) and CEIC/CBN monthly series (CEIC, 2024).
2. **2019 figure is estimated**: Unlike 2020–2024, no publisher has verified a clean full-year 2019 average in the retrieved sources. The ~11.5% figure is an estimate based on CEIC monthly data and Nairametrics reporting. Treat it as indicative.
3. **Primary vs. secondary market rates differ**: Stop rates at primary market auctions (the rate the CBN accepts bids at) may differ from secondary market yields (where existing bills are traded). Stanbic IBTC (2024) figures appear to reflect secondary market averages; NigeriaGalleria data reflects primary auction stop rates.
4. **Tenor matters**: The 91-day and 364-day tenors behaved very differently, particularly in 2021–2023. Using one without the other can be misleading.
5. **Real yields were negative for most of the period**: Nigeria's headline inflation remained elevated throughout 2019–2024. Nominal T-bill rates do not represent positive real returns for most of this period.
